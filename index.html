Is This Right For You

Who benefits most from concierge nursing

Concierge nursing is not for everyone — it's for families navigating genuinely complex medical situations where the standard system isn't keeping up.

  • Your loved one has multiple chronic conditions — dementia, Parkinson's, COPD, heart disease, diabetes, or polypharmacy
  • They take five or more medications and the management feels confusing or unsafe
  • They've been to the ER or hospital multiple times in the last year
  • They recently came home from a hospital or rehab facility and the discharge instructions were overwhelming
  • They see several specialists and nobody is coordinating the full picture
  • They show signs of cognitive decline or difficulty managing daily health tasks
  • You have a family member trying to help from out of state who needs trusted local support in Austin

Understanding Your Nurse's Credentials

DNP vs. RN — What's the Difference?

Registered Nurse (RN)

Bachelor's or Associate degree. Licensed to assess, plan, implement, and evaluate patient care. Typically 2–4 years of post-secondary education before entering clinical practice. Excellent foundational nursing care — but generalist scope.

Doctor of Nursing Practice (DNP)

Terminal clinical degree — the highest nursing education available. Equivalent in academic rigor to a medical doctorate. Focused on advanced clinical practice, systems leadership, and evidence-based care delivery. Requires 6–8 years of post-secondary education. Brings higher-level diagnostic reasoning and evidence-based protocol development to care coordination.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is concierge nursing covered by Medicare or insurance?+

These services are private pay and are not typically covered by Medicare or traditional insurance. Some clients use HSA or FSA funds. Sarah discusses all costs transparently during the free initial consultation before any commitment is made.

How quickly can care start after a hospital discharge?+

Rapid Response can mobilize quickly for post-discharge situations. Contact Sarah before discharge when possible — the earlier the engagement, the more prepared the transition will be. Post-discharge contacts are also welcomed.

What areas does Rapid Response serve?+

Rapid Response serves Austin and surrounding communities including Cedar Park, Round Rock, Georgetown, Pflugerville, Lakeway, Westlake Hills, Bee Cave, Dripping Springs, Kyle, and Buda. Mileage charges may apply for locations outside the primary service area.

Will I always work with Sarah, or does this rotate?+

You work with Sarah directly, consistently, every visit. This is the core promise of the practice. She knows your loved one's full medical picture and maintains continuity throughout the engagement — unlike home health agencies that send whoever is available.

How does a concierge nurse communicate with my loved one's physicians?+

Sarah communicates directly with the medical team. If she identifies a medication change that carries interaction risk, a care transition that seems premature, or a clinical decision that doesn't align with the patient's full medical picture, she raises it directly with the prescribing physician. This advocacy function is central to the practice.

What is the difference between concierge nursing and a home health agency?+

Home health agencies typically send whoever is available on a given day. Concierge nursing means you work with the same credentialed professional every time. Sarah has 14+ years of clinical experience and a DNP degree — not a short training program. She knows your full medical picture and coordinates across your entire care team.

What does a concierge nurse NOT do?+

Sarah is a clinical nurse advocate and advisor, not a legal decision-maker. She cannot serve as a healthcare proxy or make medical decisions on your behalf. Healthcare proxy and durable power of attorney designations must be established through legal channels. Sarah's role is to ensure the patient's documented wishes are known and respected by the care team.
